The Cartesian product of $n$ [[Set|sets]], $A_{1}, \dots, A_{n}$ is the set of all ordered $n$-tuples $(a_{1}, \dots, a_{n})$

## Notation

We can denote the Cartesian product of $n$ sets $A_{1},\dots,A_{n}$ as follows:

$$
A_{1} \times \dots \times A_{n}=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})~|~a_{i}\in A_{i}~~~\forall_{i} \in \{1,\dots,n\}\}
$$

## Example 

Suppose we have sets $A=\{1,2\}$ and $B=\{3,4\}$. The Cartesian product of $A$ and $B$ can be found by finding the set ordered pairs of the elements in $A$ and $B$. In this case, since we have two sets we can represent the ordered pairs using an array:

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}  
& 1 & 2 \\  
\hline  
3 & (1,3) & (2,3) \\  
4 & (1,4) & (2,4) \\  
\end{array}
$$

Thus:

$$
A \times B = \{(1,3), (2,3), (1,4), (2,4)\}
$$
