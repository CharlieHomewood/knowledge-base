## [[Derivative (Calculus)|Differentiation]]

In differentiation, if $f(x)=x^{n}$, then the derivative $f^{\prime}(x)$ is given by:

$$
f^{\prime}(x)=nx^{n-1}~~~~~~~~~~\forall x>0,~n \in \mathbb{R}
$$

## [[Integral|Integration]]

In integration, if $f(x)=x^{n}$, then the integral, with respect to $x$, is given by:

$$
\int x^{n}~dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }n \neq -1
$$

Where $C$ is the constant of integration.

In the case when $n=-1$, the integral, with respect to $x$, evaluates to the natural logarithm (plus $C$):

$$
\int x^{-1}~dx=\int \frac{1}{x}~dx=\ln|x|+C
$$
