An ad hoc hypothesis is an additional explanatory factor which is added to a theory to protect that theory from potentially falsifying evidence.

For example, we could posit a theory that unicorns exist. A critic may argue that there are no empirical observations of unicorns and so this would constitute evidence against the theory. However, an ad hoc hypothesis could be constructed such as "unicorns cannot be observed by humans" which makes the theory somewhat resistant to the falsifying evidence as it enables the theory to account for that evidence.

One potential solution to ad hoc hypotheses is the value of [[Parsimony|parsimony]], whereby scientific theories should involve as few assumptions as possible. Parsimony would therefore favour a theory which is also consistent with the evidence but relies on fewer assumptions (e.g. unicorns do not exist).
