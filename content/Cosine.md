Cosine is a trigonometric function which maps the value of an angle to a [[Real Numbers|real number]] between $0$ and $1$.

## Formulae

### Right-Angled Triangles

The cosine of an angle $\theta$ on a triangle can be determined by dividing the adjacent length (the side next to the angle $\theta$ which isn't the hypotenuse) by the length of the hypotenuse (the longest side of the triangle):

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
$$

### [[Vector|Vectors]]

In the context of a [[Vector Space|vector space]], the cosine of the angle between two vectors $u$ and $v$ can be determined as the [[Dot Product|dot product]] of the vectors, divided by the the product of the [[Norm|norms]] of each vector:

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{u \cdot v}{|u||v|}
$$

### [[Taylor Series]]

The cosine of $x$ can also be expressed as a Taylor series:

$$
\cos(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n)!}x^{2n}=1-\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\frac{x^{4}}{4!}-\frac{x^{6}}{6!}+\dots
$$

### [[Complex Numbers]]

The cosine of $\theta$ may also be defined using complex numbers, as half of the sum of Euler's formula with its inverse:

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}
$$
