Parsimony is a scientific value in which, all else equal, a simpler theory or model is preferred over a more complex theory or model. Thus a theory is more parsimonious than another if it relies on fewer assumptions, all else being equal.

## Relation to [[Falsifiability]]

A scientific theory can always adopt additional [[Ad Hoc Hypothesis|auxiliary hypotheses]] to protect it from being falsified by empirical evidence. Thus, to mitigate against this issue, adhering to parsimony helps to ensure our theories are expressed in their simplest forms and so are more likely to be open to falsification.
